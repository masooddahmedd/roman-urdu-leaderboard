11. Taiwan's history began in the fifteenth century, when European sailors passing by recorded the island's name as Ilha Formosa, meaning "Beautiful Island." In 1624, the Dutch East India Company established a base in southwestern Taiwan, began changing indigenous agricultural practices, and employed Chinese workers on its rice and sugar plantations. In 1683, forces of the Qing Dynasty (1644–1912) took control of Taiwan's western and eastern coastal regions, and in 1885 Taiwan was declared a province of the Qing Empire. In 1895, following defeat in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895), the Qing government signed the Treaty of Shimonoseki, under which it ceded sovereignty over Taiwan to Japan, which ruled the island until 1945.
