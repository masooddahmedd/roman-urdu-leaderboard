1. A hospitality exchange network is an organization that connects travelers with local residents in the cities they plan to visit. Joining such a network usually requires filling out an online form, although some networks offer or require additional verification. A list of available hosts is then provided in print or online, sometimes including references and reviews from other travelers. Computer programmer Casey Fenton founded Couchsurfing in January 2004 after finding a cheap flight to Iceland but being unable to find suitable accommodation. He emailed students at a local university and received an overwhelming number of offers of free accommodation.
2. In France, voting has traditionally been a low-tech process. Voters enter a voting booth, mark their preferred candidate on a preprinted paper ballot, and place it in an envelope. After officials verify the voter's identity, the voter puts the envelope into the ballot box and signs the electoral register. French electoral law strictly regulates these procedures. Since 1988, ballot boxes have been required to be transparent so that voters and observers can see that no envelopes are present at the beginning of voting and that no envelopes are added except those of authorized voters, which are carefully counted. Candidates have the right to send representatives to monitor every stage of the process. In the evening, volunteers count the votes under strict supervision and according to specific procedures.

5. Backpacking on skis across snow is also known as backcountry skiing, ski touring, or ski hiking. It is related to, but generally does not include, Alpine-style ski touring or mountaineering, the latter being practiced on steep terrain and requiring stiffer skis and boots. Think of a skiing route in much the same way as a hiking or walking route. In good conditions, you can cover considerably more distance than you would on foot, but you will rarely achieve the speed of cross-country skiing on prepared tracks while carrying a heavy backpack.

10. Tutankhamun's Tomb (KV62) — Discovered by Howard Carter in 1922, the burial site of the young king is perhaps one of the most famous tombs in the Valley of the Kings. Compared with most other royal tombs, however, Tutankhamun's tomb is very small, has limited decoration, and is barely worth visiting. Anyone hoping to examine the damage caused during attempts to remove the mummy from its coffin will be disappointed, as only the head and shoulders are visible. The valuable artifacts that made the tomb famous are no longer there, having been transferred to the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. Visitors with limited time would be better off spending it elsewhere.
11. Canyoning involves descending into a river gorge, which may be either dry or filled with water. Canyoning combines elements of swimming, climbing, and jumping, but it requires relatively little training and physical fitness to get started, especially compared with activities such as rock climbing, scuba diving, or Alpine skiing.

14. After Congress adopted it on July 4, a handwritten draft signed by Congress President John Hancock and Secretary Charles Thomson was sent a few blocks away to John Dunlap's printing shop. Between 150 and 200 copies were produced overnight, now known as the "Dunlap Broadsides." The document was first read publicly by John Nixon in the courtyard of Independence Hall on July 8. A copy was sent to George Washington on July 6, and he had it read aloud to his troops in New York on July 9. One copy reached London on August 10. The 25 Dunlap Broadsides known to survive are the oldest existing copies of the document. The original manuscript has been lost.
15. Immediately after hostilities began, Britain imposed a naval blockade on Germany. This strategy proved effective, cutting off vital military and civilian supplies, although the blockade violated generally accepted principles of international law established through international treaties over previous centuries. Britain laid mines throughout international waters to prevent ships from entering large areas of the sea, thereby endangering neutral vessels as well. Because the response to this tactic was very limited, Germany expected that unrestricted submarine warfare would provoke a similarly limited reaction.
16. A backcountry permit is required to camp below the rim of the Grand Canyon. Permits are limited to protect the canyon and become available on the first day of the month, four months before the month in which the trip begins. For example, backcountry permits for any starting date in May become available on January 1. The most popular locations, such as Bright Angel Campground near Phantom Ranch, are usually fully booked through applications received on the first day reservations open. A limited number of permits are reserved for walk-in applications and are issued on a first-come, first-served basis.
17. Scientists have discovered geological formations called lobate scarps on the Moon's surface, which have formed very slowly as the Moon contracts. These features, found across the lunar surface, show little evidence of weathering. This suggests that the geological events responsible for their formation occurred relatively recently. The findings challenge the claim that the Moon is completely geologically inactive.
18. The South Pole Traverse, also known as the South Pole Highway, is a 1,600-kilometer route from McMurdo Station on the Ross Sea to the South Pole. Its path is marked by flags attached to poles driven into the snow. Only specialized tractors pulling sledges loaded with fuel and supplies can travel along this route. These vehicles are very slow, so the traverse must make a long detour around the Transantarctic Mountains to reach the plateau.
19. There are several theories about how Sanskrit originated. One suggests that the Aryans migrated to India from the west, bringing their language with them. Sanskrit is an ancient language comparable to Latin, which was spoken in Europe. The world's oldest known book was written in Sanskrit. After the Upanishads were compiled, Sanskrit declined because of the influence of religious leaders. Sanskrit is an extremely complex and rich language that served as the source of several modern Indian languages, just as Latin provided the foundation for European languages such as French and Spanish.
