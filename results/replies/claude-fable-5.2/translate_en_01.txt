4. Dr. Tony Moll discovered extremely drug-resistant tuberculosis (XDR-TB) in the South African region of KwaZulu-Natal. 5. NextGen is a system that, according to the FAA’s claims, allows aircraft to fly shorter routes, saving millions of gallons of fuel every year and cutting carbon emissions. It uses satellite-based technology, as opposed to the older ground-radar-based technology, so that air traffic controllers can pinpoint aircraft with greater precision and pilots can be given more accurate information.

9. Greenland is well connected, with 93 percent of the population having internet access, thanks to undersea fibre-optic cable links to Europe and broadband satellite. Your hotel or host (if you are staying in a guesthouse) will likely have Wi-Fi or an internet-connected PC, and all settlements will have an internet café or places with public Wi-Fi. As mentioned above, the word “Eskimo” is acceptable in the United States, but many non-Americans, especially in Canada, consider it an offensive word. Although you may hear the word used by Greenland locals, foreigners should avoid using it. The native inhabitants of Greenland call themselves Inuit in Canada and Greenlanders or Kalaalleq (plural Kalaallit) in Greenland. Crime, and ill will toward foreigners in general, is virtually unknown in Greenland. Even in the towns, there are no “rough areas.” Cold weather is perhaps the only real danger an unprepared person will face. If you want to visit Greenland in winter (bearing in mind that the further north you go, the colder it will be), it is essential that you bring enough warm clothing. The long days of summer can lead to problems getting enough sleep and related health issues. In summer, also watch out for the Nordic mosquitoes. Although they do not transmit any disease, their bites can be irritating.

16. Keep an eye on your bags when others are carrying your luggage, especially when crossing international borders. You may find that you have been made a drug carrier without your knowledge, which could land you in a great deal of trouble. This includes waiting in line, as sniffer dogs can be used at any time without notice. Some countries have extremely harsh punishments even for first-time offenders; these include up to 10 years in prison or the death penalty. Unattended bags are a target for thieves and can also attract the attention of authorities wary of bomb threats.
17. All nouns, along with the word Sie for “you”, always begin with a capital letter, even in the middle of a sentence. This is an important way of distinguishing between some verbs and objects. It also arguably makes reading easier, although it makes writing quite difficult, since you have to work out whether a verb or adjective is being used in its basic form or not.

19. The 800-mile Trans-Alaska Pipeline System was shut down after thousands of barrels of crude oil spilled south of Fairbanks, Alaska. The 800-mile Trans-Alaska Pipeline System was shut down after thousands of barrels of crude oil spilled south of Fairbanks, Alaska. The opening of the valves allowed a pressure release for the system, and the oil flowed over a pad into a tank that can hold 55,000 barrels (2.3 million gallons). As of Wednesday afternoon, the tank’s vents were still leaking, probably because of excessive vapor build-up inside the tank. Another secondary containment area beneath the tanks, capable of holding 104,500 barrels, has not yet been filled to capacity.
20. For a long time during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, it was believed that the first inhabitants of New Zealand were the Maori people, who hunted large birds called moas. The theory then established the idea that the Maori people had migrated from Polynesia in a great fleet and taken New Zealand from the Moriori people to establish an agricultural society. However, new evidence suggests that the Moriori were a group of mainland Maori who migrated from New Zealand to the Chatham Islands, where they developed their own distinctive, peaceful culture. There was also another tribe living on the Chatham Islands, Maori who had migrated away from New Zealand. They called themselves Moriori; there were several skirmishes between them, and in the end the Moriori were wiped out.
