In France, voting has traditionally been a low-tech experience: voters isolate themselves in a voting booth and put a pre-printed sheet of paper indicating their candidate of choice into an envelope. After officials verify the voter’s identity, the voter drops the envelope into the ballot box and signs the voting roll. French electoral law rather strictly codifies these proceedings. Since 1988, ballot boxes must be transparent so that voters and observers can see that no envelopes are present at the start of the vote and that no envelopes are added except those of duly counted and authorized voters. Candidates have the right to send representatives to witness every part of the process. In the evening, volunteers count the votes under strict supervision, following specific procedures.

5. Backpacking by ski: this activity is also called backcountry ski, ski touring or ski hiking. It is related to, but usually does not involve, alpine-style ski touring or mountaineering, the latter being done in steep terrain and requiring much stiffer skis and boots. Think of a skiing route as you would think of a hiking route. In good conditions you will be able to cover somewhat greater distances than by walking, but only very rarely will you reach the speeds of cross-country skiing without a heavy backpack on groomed tracks.

10. Tutankhamun’s tomb (KV62): KV62 may be the most famous of the tombs in the Valley, the scene of Howard Carter’s 1922 discovery of the almost intact royal burial of the young king. Compared with most of the other royal tombs, however, Tutankhamun’s tomb is barely worth visiting, being much smaller and with limited decoration. Anyone interested in seeing evidence of the damage done to the mummy during attempts to remove it from the coffin will only be disappointed, as only the head and shoulders are visible. The tomb’s fabulous riches are no longer in it, having been moved to the Egyptian Museum in Cairo. Visitors with limited time would do best to spend it elsewhere.

After its adoption by Congress on July 4, a handwritten draft signed by the President of Congress, John Hancock, and the Secretary, Charles Thomson, was sent a few blocks away to the printing shop of John Dunlap. Through the night between 150 and 200 copies were made, now known as “Dunlap broadsides”. The first public reading of the document was by John Nixon in the yard of Independence Hall on July 8. One was sent to George Washington on July 6, who had it read to his troops in New York on July 9. A copy reached London on August 10. The 25 Dunlap broadsides still known to exist are the oldest surviving copies of the document. The original handwritten copy has not survived.
15. Soon after the outbreak of hostilities, Britain began a naval blockade of Germany. The strategy proved effective, cutting off vital military and civilian supplies, although the blockade violated generally accepted principles of international law codified by international agreements over the previous centuries. Britain mined international waters to prevent any ships from entering entire sections of ocean, endangering even neutral ships. Since there was only a limited response to this tactic, Germany expected a similar response to its unrestricted submarine warfare.
16. Any camping below the rim in the Grand Canyon requires a backcountry permit. Permits are limited in order to protect the canyon, and they become available on the first day of the month, four months before the starting month. Thus, a backcountry permit for any start date in May becomes available on January 1. Space in the most popular areas, such as the Bright Angel Campground adjacent to Phantom Ranch, is generally filled by the requests received on the first date they are opened for reservations. A limited number of permits are reserved for walk-in requests, available on a first-come, first-served basis.
17. Scientists have discovered landforms scattered across the Moon’s surface called lobate scarps, which have apparently resulted from the Moon shrinking very slowly. These scarps were found all over the Moon and appear to be minimally weathered, indicating that the geologic events that created them were fairly recent. This theory contradicts the claim that the Moon is completely devoid of geologic activity.
18. The South Pole Traverse (or Highway) is a 1,600-kilometer road from McMurdo Station on the Ross Sea to the Pole. It is compacted snow with the crevasses filled in and marked by flags. It can only be traveled by specialized tractors hauling sleds loaded with fuel and supplies. These are not very nimble, so the trail has to take a long swerve around the Transantarctic Mountains to come onto the plateau.
19. There are several theories about how Sanskrit came into existence. One of them concerns an Aryan migration from the west into India, with the Aryans bringing their language with them. Sanskrit is an ancient language, comparable to the Latin spoken in Europe. The earliest known book in the world was written in Sanskrit. After the compilation of the Upanishads, Sanskrit faded because of the religious leaders. Sanskrit is a very complex and rich language, which has served as the source for many modern Indian languages, just as Latin is the source for European languages such as French and Spanish.
