4. Lions are the most social of the cats, living in large groups called prides. A pride consists of one to three related adult males, along with as many as thirty females and cubs. The females are usually very closely related to one another, being a large family of sisters and daughters. Lion prides act much like packs of wolves or dogs, animals surprisingly similar to lions (but not to other big cats) in behaviour, and likewise very deadly to their prey.

11. Taiwan’s story begins back in the 15th century, when European sailors passing by recorded the island’s name as Ilha Formosa, or “beautiful island”. In 1624, the Dutch East India Company established a base in southwestern Taiwan, initiating a transformation in indigenous grain production practices and employing Chinese labourers to work on its rice and sugar plantations. In 1683, forces of the Qing dynasty (1644–1912) took control of Taiwan’s western and northern coastal areas, and in 1885 declared Taiwan a province of the Qing Empire. In 1895, after defeat in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895), the Qing government signed the Treaty of Shimonoseki, by which it ceded sovereignty over Taiwan to Japan, which ruled the island until 1945.

17. A World’s Fair (commonly called a World Exposition, or simply Expo) is a large international festival of arts and sciences. Participating countries present artistic and educational displays in national pavilions to showcase world issues or their country’s culture and history. International Horticultural Expositions are specialised events showcasing floral displays, botanical gardens and anything else to do with plants. Although in theory they can take place annually (so long as they are in different countries), in practice they do not. These events normally last anywhere between three and six months, and are held on sites no smaller than 50 hectares.
